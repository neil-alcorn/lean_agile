# AI-Era Methodology Naming Exploration

## Goal

Find a one-word methodology name for the book's new AI-era operating method that feels more like **Agile** or **Lean** than like a consulting slogan, vendor product, or certification scheme.

## Naming Constraints

The name should:

- be one word
- be short and easy to say
- avoid sounding cute or gimmicky
- avoid obvious product/consultancy framing
- support a broad operating method, not just one tool or role
- feel credible in both executive and practitioner contexts
- be simple enough to anchor a book title or subtitle

## Naming Families Considered

### 1. Enterprise phrase names

Examples:

- Agentic DevOps
- AI-Led SDLC
- AI-Driven Development
- Context-Driven Development

Why reject as primary method names:

- too many syllables
- already in market circulation
- too generic to own
- sound more like category labels than like a movement or working method

These remain useful as market language and research anchors, but not as the book's methodology brand.

### 2. Noun/metaphor names

Examples considered:

- Signal
- Pulse
- Thread
- Relay
- Mesh

Why this family is risky:

- noun names can be memorable, but many short nouns already have heavy collisions in software or product markets
- they can become brandable, but they usually need more explanation than adjective-style names
- some of the best nouns are already occupied or too tool-like

Conclusion:

possible fallback family, but not the best first move

### 3. Ordinary adjective names

Examples from history:

- Agile
- Lean

Why this family fits:

- familiar pattern
- readable and repeatable
- broad enough to grow
- does not force artificial jargon
- lets the methodology feel like a way of working rather than a product

Conclusion:

This is the preferred naming family.

## Current Market Terms To Avoid As The Book's Core Name

### Agentic DevOps

Already used heavily by Microsoft and GitHub ecosystem material.

Useful as evidence that the market is moving. Bad as a distinctive book method name.

### Spec-Driven Development

Already in active use as an AI-development methodology label.

### Intent-Driven Development

Already in active use.

### Context Mesh

Already in active use.

### Anchored / Anchored Development

Already in use as a framework name for AI-assisted development documentation and artifact discipline.

### Grounded / Grounded AI

Strong conceptually, but already heavily used in AI safety, AI reliability, and AI implementation language.

### Adaptive

Semantically viable, but already long established in adjacent software and business contexts. Too much legacy ambiguity.

### Vibe

Culturally real, commercially tempting, strategically wrong for this book.

It suggests looseness where the method needs trust, rigor, and accountability.

## Shortlist Worth Arguing About

This is not the final naming decision. It is the first shortlist that survives the current constraint set.

### 1. Guided

Why it works:

- simple
- two syllables
- clear human-in-the-loop implication
- works for business and software audiences
- reinforces the argument that human judgment moves upstream

Why it may fail:

- may sound too soft if the supporting language is not strong
- needs the surrounding methodology architecture to supply rigor

Collision screen:

- no strong evidence yet of an established software methodology called `Guided`
- phrase-level collisions exist in generic product marketing, but not a clear dominant methodology collision was found in current quick screening

Status:

**promising**

### 2. Steady

Why it works:

- short
- calm
- implies controlled speed rather than chaos
- fits the book's challenge to velocity theater
- works well with ITIL and service reliability ideas

Why it may fail:

- could sound too conservative if not paired with a strong bottleneck-shift story
- less obviously AI-native than some alternatives

Collision screen:

- no strong software methodology collision found in current quick screening

Status:

**promising**

### 3. Aligned

Why it works:

- strong link to one of the book's deepest recurring themes
- good cross-functional resonance
- easy executive appeal

Why it may fail:

- too much business-speak baggage
- company-name collisions exist
- may sound like a consulting program rather than a delivery method

Collision screen:

- visible company/service collisions
- no dominant methodology collision located, but semantic clutter is high

Status:

**possible, but weaker**

### 4. Grounded

Why it works:

- excellent semantic fit for AI-era trust, context, and reality-based delivery
- strongly contrasts with vibe coding
- supports the method's seriousness

Why it may fail:

- already crowded in AI safety and AI implementation discourse
- may sound more like a safety posture than a delivery method

Collision screen:

- strong collisions in AI implementation, safety, and consulting language

Status:

**conceptually strong, commercially messy**

## Rejected Or Lower-Confidence Options

### Anchored

Rejected because `Anchored Development` already exists as an AI-assisted development framework.

### Adaptive

Rejected as a lead candidate because it is already too established and too broad in adjacent methodology language.

### Clear

Rejected because it collides with multiple AI/business products and sounds too generic.

### Context / Intent / Spec variants

Rejected because they are either already occupied or too jargon-forward.

## Naming Principles For Supporting Terms

The methodology name can be new. Most supporting terms should not be.

### Keep familiar where possible

Prefer to keep:

- requirements
- backlog
- stakeholder
- architect
- governance
- acceptance criteria
- gate
- release
- service management
- business analysis
- project management
- agent
- CLI
- MCP

### Only rename when the concept is truly changed

New terms are justifiable only when AI changes the nature of the thing itself.

Likely valid examples:

- living spec
- audience impact

Likely invalid examples:

- renaming Scrum Master just to sound modern
- renaming business analysis because AI sounds fresher
- inventing new labels for ordinary approvals or coordination work

## Working Recommendation

The strongest current candidates are:

1. **Guided**
2. **Steady**

If the book needs a methodology name today, `Guided` is the best fit between clarity, simplicity, and future flexibility.

If the book wants a stronger stability-and-trust emphasis, `Steady` is the strongest alternative.

If the book wants a concept rather than a brand, `Grounded` remains powerful language for the internal thesis even if it is not the final method name.

## Recommendation For The Next Naming Pass

Do one more round focused only on short adjective candidates with low market collision and strong executive/practitioner legibility.

That round should:

- generate 15-20 candidates
- screen them for obvious software/AI collisions
- test them against chapter language and possible subtitles
- reject anything that feels like product marketing or consultant fog
