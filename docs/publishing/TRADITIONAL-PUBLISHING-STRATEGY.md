# Traditional Publishing Strategy

## Objective

Pursue a traditional publishing deal for *Lean and Agile* without self-publishing and without paying a publisher or agent up front.

## What The Current Industry Guidance Says

### The likely path

For a nonfiction business/leadership book, the usual path is:

1. prepare a strong proposal
2. identify comparable books and audience fit
3. query agents or submit to selected direct-submission publishers
4. revise with agent/editor feedback if interest develops
5. move into acquisitions if a publisher wants the project

### What major-house guidance implies

The biggest houses still mostly expect agented submissions. Penguin Random House says that for most cases, an agent is still "required and advisable" for major-house publishing, and it points nonfiction authors first toward completing a manuscript or proposal before querying.

### What nonfiction guidance implies

For nonfiction, the proposal is not a side document. It is the business case for the book. The proposal has to prove:

- there is a real market
- this book has a clear angle
- the author has authority
- the author can help reach readers

### What scam guidance implies

Anything that asks for money up front should be treated as suspect. Real agents earn commissions on sales. Charging mandatory fees before representation or submission is not standard practice.

## What This Means For This Book

This book is not a niche technical manual and not a pure memoir. It sits in the business / leadership / organizational change / AI-at-work space. That is commercially promising, but it is also crowded.

That means we will need to be sharp about:

- audience definition
- differentiation
- practical authority
- market timing
- author platform

The current strongest commercial angle is not "here is another book about Agile."

It is closer to:

> In the age of AI, the names of the methodologies keep changing, but the underlying truths of quality, trust, systems thinking, and leadership have not.

That angle helps the book feel timely without becoming hype-driven.

## What To Do

### Do

- build the proposal and manuscript in parallel
- sharpen the audience to something more specific than "leaders"
- collect platform evidence now
- keep a current list of comps published in roughly the last two years
- target agents and publishers that already handle business, leadership, organizational change, work culture, technology, or AI-adjacent nonfiction
- follow each submission guideline exactly
- keep a clean contact and submission log

### Do Not

- submit too early with a mushy angle
- mass-query with one generic pitch
- claim the book is for "everyone"
- claim there are no comparable titles
- pay anyone up front for "representation"
- assume a good manuscript alone is enough in business nonfiction

## Recommended Submission Sequence

### Phase 1: Internal package

Build these first:

- proposal overview
- audience statement
- competitive title analysis
- author/platform section
- chapter summaries
- two or three sample chapters
- marketing/publicity outline

### Phase 2: Targeting

Create two parallel lists:

- agents
- direct-submission publishers

For each target, capture:

- why the target is a fit
- exact submission route
- exact requested materials
- any evidence of business/leadership/nonfiction appetite
- whether the target is open, closed, or not suitable

### Phase 3: Outreach

Start with a narrow first wave.

Recommended first wave:

- 5 to 8 well-matched agents
- 1 to 3 direct-submission publishers with strong fit

Do not start with a huge blast. The first batch should teach us what works.

## What We Need To Strengthen Before Submission

### 1. Positioning

We need a crisp one-sentence answer to:

- why this book
- why now
- why you

### 2. Platform

We need a real inventory of:

- speaking
- workshops
- consulting
- leadership roles
- audience reach
- newsletter or LinkedIn presence
- related products, tools, or frameworks
- bulk-buy possibilities through organizations

### 3. Proposal package

We should assume that agents and publishers will want:

- clear audience
- recent comps
- polished sample chapters
- platform evidence
- realistic marketing commitments

### 4. Citations and permissions

As the manuscript develops, we should keep track of:

- quotations
- adapted frameworks
- image or diagram sources
- permissions risk

That will make the project look more professional later and prevent cleanup pain.

## Recommended Operating Rule

For now, we should treat the outreach package as its own product line inside the repo.

That means:

- every publishing-facing claim should be evidence-backed
- every target should be validated
- every draft pitch should be iterated, not improvised live
