# Pitch Drafts

These are early working drafts, not final outreach copy.

The goal right now is to develop strong positioning language that we can later adapt into:

- query letters
- proposal overviews
- publisher submission forms
- verbal pitches

## One-Sentence Positioning

### Draft 1

*Lean and Agile* is a business book about how leaders can use AI without losing the deeper truths of quality, trust, systems thinking, and human dignity that have always governed good work.

### Draft 2

*Lean and Agile* shows that the modern alphabet soup of improvement frameworks is less a set of rival religions than a family of recurring truths about leadership, learning, quality, and process, now tested again in the age of AI.

### Draft 3

For leaders and practitioners drowning in jargon, reorgs, and AI hype, *Lean and Agile* translates Lean, Agile, ITIL, Six Sigma, and Deming back into plain English and usable wisdom.

### Draft 4

*Lean and Agile* is a leadership book for the AI era that shows why better tools still fail inside bad systems, and how enduring principles of trust, quality, and process can keep modern organizations from automating their confusion.

## Short Elevator Pitch

Most organizations treat Lean, Agile, Six Sigma, ITIL, and AI transformation as separate conversations. This book argues that they are all wrestling with the same underlying questions: How do people do good work? How do leaders remove barriers instead of adding them? How do systems create quality, fear, waste, or pride? And what changes when AI enters the room?

Written with practical clarity, humane skepticism, and a little workplace wit, *Lean and Agile* helps readers see the enduring principles beneath the jargon and apply them without losing their humanity.

## Hard-Nosed Assessment

What is working in the pitch:

- the AI-era timing
- the anti-jargon promise
- the humane systems angle

What is still weak:

- we still have not named the primary reader tightly enough
- the phrase "family resemblance between frameworks" is smart but not yet commercial
- the current pitch still risks sounding broad instead of inevitable

The pitch gets stronger when it answers:

- what pain is the reader in
- what bad instinct will this book save them from
- what can they do better after reading it

## Publisher-Facing Framing

This is not a certification manual and not an AI hype book. It is a practical business and leadership book with an IT center of gravity and broad organizational relevance. Its commercial angle comes from combining:

- enduring process and leadership principles
- current AI-era urgency
- cross-framework clarity
- a readable, distinctive voice

## Agent-Facing Framing

This project is timely because it speaks to a widespread organizational problem: leaders are being pushed to modernize faster, adopt AI faster, and "transform" faster, while many of the underlying failures in trust, process, incentives, and quality remain unchanged. The book offers a unifying frame that makes familiar methodologies newly legible and newly useful.

## Early Query Hook Direction

Possible opening angle:

> Every few years, organizations rename the same hopes and dysfunctions. They call it Lean, Agile, Six Sigma, ITIL, digital transformation, or now AI enablement. The labels change faster than the underlying problems. *Lean and Agile* is a practical business book about the deeper truths underneath those labels, and how leaders can use them without losing trust, judgment, or the people doing the work.

## Things The Pitch Must Eventually Prove

- who the primary reader is
- why the book is needed now
- what makes it different from existing business / AI / leadership books
- why you are the right person to write it
- what audience or platform can help carry it

## Things To Avoid In Final Pitching

- "This book is for everyone"
- "There is nothing else like it"
- "AI will change everything" without qualification
- generic consultant phrasing
- long methodology taxonomy before the hook is clear
- making the book sound more abstract than it really is
- making the book sound nicer than it is useful

## What We Still Need Before Final Pitches

- sharper subtitle options
- better evidence of platform and reach
- recent comp titles
- sample chapter selection
- a cleaner single-paragraph author credential summary
- a more ruthless articulation of the exact reader problem
