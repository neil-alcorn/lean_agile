# Deming History Fact Check for Chapter 1

Status: editorial research supporting a proposed Edit Copy section; not canonical manuscript text.

## Findings

- **“Rejected in America” is too strong.** Deming worked for the U.S. government and taught statistical methods in the United States. The defensible formulation is that American management largely overlooked or failed to broadly adopt his management philosophy until his belated recognition around 1980.
- **Japan invited Deming in 1950.** The Japanese Union of Scientists and Engineers (JUSE) invited him to lecture on statistical quality control to engineers and business leaders.
- **The Deming Prize began in 1951 and continues.** JUSE established it in 1951, and JUSE continues to publish annual winners and award ceremonies.
- **Toyota received the Deming Application Prize in 1965.** Toyota’s corporate history also documents its movement from inspection toward building quality into the process.
- **American recognition accelerated in 1980.** NBC aired *If Japan Can, Why Can’t We?* on June 24, 1980, bringing Deming to a broad U.S. audience.
- **Japanese automobile and electronics competition is relevant but multi-causal.** By the mid-1980s, Japan was an economic powerhouse and U.S. quality performance was widely viewed as lagging. Deming was an important influence, not the sole cause of Japan’s success.
- **TPS should not be attributed solely to Deming.** Toyota’s just-in-time and jidoka foundations developed within Toyota. Deming and total quality control influenced the surrounding quality-management system.
