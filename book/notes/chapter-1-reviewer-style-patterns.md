# Chapter 1 Human Review Style Patterns

## Purpose

Use these patterns when editing later chapters. They are derived from the detailed human review of Chapter 1 and should supplement `book/STYLE-AND-INTENT.md`.

## Editing Priorities

1. Prefer the shortest complete thought. If a sentence reaches its point, remove the trailing qualifier rather than explaining it again.
2. Split overloaded sentences. A clean period often reads more naturally than an em dash, semicolon, or rhetorical aside.
3. Remove unnecessary em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon, semicolon, or parentheses based on the sentence’s actual logic.
4. Italicize a character’s internal thoughts. Keep the attribution outside the italics when appropriate: `*Two more?* she thought.`
5. Keep point of view consistent. Do not drift from third person into “we” or “our” unless the author is intentionally addressing the reader.
6. Repair fragments and clunky openings rather than preserving them because the intended meaning can be inferred.
7. Favor direct language over polished-sounding rhetorical flourishes. Avoid constructions that announce their cleverness or resemble generic AI cadence.
8. Preserve conversational wit when it earns its place, but do not let an aside interrupt the argument.
9. Use Markdown headings and emphasis as production instructions, not decoration. Keep them when they create real structure or emphasis; remove them when they do not.
10. Treat reviewer notes as diagnostic instructions. Resolve the underlying concern in the prose, then remove the note from the manuscript.

## Chapter-Level Review Checklist

- Does every paragraph advance one clear idea?
- Can the last phrase or clause be removed without losing meaning?
- Are any em dashes doing work that simpler punctuation would do better?
- Are internal thoughts italicized consistently?
- Does the point of view remain stable?
- Do headings help the reader navigate the argument?
- Is bold or italic emphasis intentional and sparse?
- Does any sentence sound unusually polished, symmetrical, explanatory, or formulaic compared with the surrounding human voice?
- Have all reviewer notes been resolved rather than copied into the prose?
